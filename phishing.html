Phishing via QR Codes

How attackers steal your credentials with a single scan

How QR Phishing Works

Attackers place fake QR codes in public places (e.g., on parking meters, flyers, or restaurant tables). When you scan them, you're redirected to a website that looks exactly like a legitimate login page (bank, email, social media).

  • The trap: The URL is slightly misspelled (e.g., bankk-login.com instead of bank-login.com).
  • The harvest: You enter your username and password, which are sent directly to the attacker.
  • The aftermath: Your account is compromised, and the attacker can steal funds, identity, or sell your data.

Real‑world example: In 2023, a wave of QR phishing scams targeted parking payment apps in major cities, netting thousands of dollars from unsuspecting drivers.
